Notes on building in public

15 November 2026 · 5 min read

  • learning
  • process

Sharing unfinished work feels strange at first. The instinct is to wait until everything is polished, tested, and impossible to criticise. But waiting has a cost: you lose the conversations that would have made the work better in the first place.

Early is useful

When you share something early, you get feedback while it's still cheap to act on. A question about a confusing label is easy to fix when the label is still a variable name. Six months later, it's a migration.

Early posts also give you a trail of decisions. Six months from now, you may not remember why you chose a particular approach. A short note from the day you made the choice is surprisingly valuable.

You don't have to perform

"Building in public" has become associated with metrics, threads, and a kind of relentless optimism that doesn't match most people's actual experience. It doesn't have to be that way. It can simply mean writing down what you did and what you learned.

A bad week is still information. So is a refactor that didn't help, or an idea you abandoned after two hours.

A lightweight habit

Here's the version I'm trying to keep sustainable:

  1. Write one paragraph about what changed this week.
  2. Add one sentence about what surprised you.
  3. Publish it, even if it's short.

That's it. No chart, no grand conclusion, no promise to post every day. The goal is not to build an audience — it's to build a habit of noticing.

The side effect

The unexpected benefit is that writing about a problem often helps solve it. Explaining something forces you to make the details explicit, and the gaps become obvious. More than once I've found the fix halfway through a paragraph.

So this blog is partly an archive and partly a thinking tool. If you're reading this and recognising a problem I've described, get in touch. That's the whole point of putting it out here.
